Private AI

Concept and pricing brief for firms that keep client data in the building.

1. Why this exists

Every mainstream AI tool runs on someone else's computer. Your transcripts, client files, and privileged documents travel to a vendor's cloud and get processed under terms the vendor can change.

For many businesses that trade is fine. For a law firm, a wealth advisor, or a clinic, it is the whole problem. Privileged material is privileged because it stays with you.

There is a second cost. API bills are metered. Every document and every request adds to a number you discover at the end of the month.

The system. A machine you own, in your office, running the AI workloads that touch sensitive data. The data never leaves the building. The bill is the hardware plus a flat retainer.

One honest number first. At a small firm's volume, metered API pricing often wins on raw dollars, and the assessment shows you that math. Firms buy this for the privacy, and for a bill that stays flat while usage grows.

2. What runs on your hardware

A 27B-class open model covers the work that consumes most of a firm's AI spend:

  • Transcription. Calls, depositions, dictation. An open speech model on the same machine turns audio into text, and the recording never leaves the office. Measured on our own hardware: 71 minutes of courtroom audio transcribed in 98 seconds, checked word for word against the official transcript (October 2026).
  • Summarization and extraction. Long documents in, structured facts out.
  • Classification and routing. Intake, triage, tagging.
  • Search across your own files. Ask questions of your document base and get cited answers.

The hybrid, for everything else. Frontier reasoning stays a cloud job, and some work genuinely needs it. For that work the local box scrubs names, matters, and identifying details first. The clean text goes to the cloud. The privileged part stays home. We built this exact pattern for a dental scribe product.

3. We run this ourselves

Our lab machine is an NVIDIA DGX Spark serving a quantized 27B model in a container, reachable only from our own devices. Every claim below is a measured result from that box, and the methodology travels with every build.

  • Speed. 48 to 58 tokens per second on code, about 25 on prose. First token in under 0.3 seconds.
  • Reliability. A 50-sequence tool-call battery scored by an independent judge: 50 of 50 clean, on two separate runs.
  • Security. Access runs over a private Tailscale network behind an identity proxy. A request without the right login gets a 403 and a log line.
  • Acceptance. A five-brief benchmark suite scores every configuration change, so "faster" is always a measured number.

The box has also gone down: breaker trips, one memory freeze. That history is why the third tier below exists.

The assessment picks the rung. Every machine is sized from measured throughput, and you purchase directly from the vendor.
